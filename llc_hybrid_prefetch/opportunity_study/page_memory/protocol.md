- Reuse the existing later BFS3 recording and the published within-recording prefix fit. The 759 versus 511 earlier matches motivated this follow-up. This is a post-hoc mechanism experiment.
- Freeze the next-touch target, service threshold, hash, interpolation, backoff, and 1-in-8 grant schedule from history/page_association.py. Compare current PC and current line offset, previous same-page offset, and previous same-page offset plus PC. Previous state must be available within 64 prior LLC ingresses and 4,096 cycles. Export counts only from the first 60% of baseline Stride ingresses. The middle 20% is unused. Use the final 20% and drop its guarded tail to match the scored population.
- Use a fixed eligible ingress-index interval defined on the original Stride replay. The interval and one data-independent slot per consecutive eight-ingress block are experimental duty-cycle controls. Each live arm counts its own ingresses. Confirm the original Stride no-action replay reproduces the frozen reference at **every** tested grant. Live arms may diverge after the first intervention.
- At one grant, propose at most one auxiliary line in the same physical page. When it matches a legal native choice, retain native attribution. Otherwise replace the native choice through the existing admission pipeline. If the auxiliary issues no choice, retain the native choice. Compare native Stride, forward next-line and repeating the previous within-page delta at the same grants. Both simple rules fall back to native when they would cross the page or predict the current line.
- Compare a fully associative 64-ingress window (exact within the cutoffs) with a fixed direct-mapped 16-entry physical-page table for the two history rules. Record direct attribution, dynamic gains and losses, DRAM bytes, head wait, and fixed-work cycles. All runs are single-active-core with original cache and memory timing. The signed time change includes the complete 2M-instruction ROI. Do not change rules or bounds after observing replay outcomes.
