# What should a learned prefetcher remember, and when should it retrieve it?

The most useful test of memory is a *deadline*. Find two triggers with the same currently affordable context, enough time for a fill, and different later targets. Then show that a distinguishable earlier event changes which physical line to request. Finally replay the decisions against an equally early conventional rule. A higher next-address score on hits after the deadline cannot settle this question.

Later BFS-3 supplies a direct test of selective retrieval. Under a common 1,460-grant opportunity, looking up the last offset for the current physical page selected 759 future offsets correctly, compared with 511 for current context. In native suffix replay, this saved 21,596 more cycles than the current-context table. Equal-grant next-line then saved a further 7,037 cycles. Two distinct target loads remained exposed under next-line, repeat-delta, multi-delta, SPP and current context, yet a 16-entry page history supplied a timely first use for each. Their prior references were only two ingresses back, 350 and 684 cycles before the decisions. The evidence favors keeping the page-indexed read as a control and searching for cases where phase-specific retrieval changes the right decision beyond next-line. [Matched native experiments](../page_memory/results.csv)

The real recordings already distinguish three problems. MCF 665B has loads that Stride can name, sometimes too late. Two-step lookahead improved its mixed execution even without a learned predictor. The sampled CactuBSSN 3477B retirement-stalling misses rarely had a same-page trigger with sufficient lead, even in hindsight. GAP BFS-8 lost timely coverage when co-running with LBM despite identical baseline miss counts. These observations call for different experiments. One concerns the issue distance, another the physical address that can be constructed, and the third shared service time. [Measurements and evidence limits](architectural_opportunities.md)

## Mechanisms worth borrowing only under specific evidence

The following are hypotheses and *illustrative* engineering budgets. No ML prefetcher was trained or simulated. The memory-model papers evaluated language or sequence tasks. Their throughput results do not determine the time or initiation interval of a cache controller.

## A decision at LLC ingress

Preserve the native Stride fast path in the first design discussion. An auxiliary path may (i) produce a different legal target, (ii) name the same target earlier, or (iii) contribute evidence for priority while a candidate can still be changed. If the first request has already left, later agreement can update feedback or reprioritize outstanding downstream service where supported. It cannot move the original issue timestamp. Merge same-line proposals in the shared physical issuer and record source bits. Run both serial wait and asynchronous queue variants with finite service capacity and bounded inference rate. A measured latency sweep can reject slow designs; a passing sweep still needs SRAM timing, synthesis and power modeling.

Before estimating performance per area, count **all** storage: weights, input embedding table, per-core state, candidate physical page tags, tracked outcomes, metadata cache, queues and arbitration bits. Count shared read/write ports and recurrent write hazards. Illustrative comparison: an 8-input / 8-hidden / 14-output dense MLP has 176 weights, 22 biases and 176 MACs per scored event; an 8-input / 8-state GRU has 384 matrix weights and approximately 384 MACs plus gates before any output head. INT8 may save storage, but accumulators need extra bits. Delayed updates require a timestamp or sequence token. Trace peak arrivals and deadline slack; simulate actual queuing of inference, stale-candidate cancellation and page ownership checks. Validate integer saturation, wrong-path/phase resets and cross-core separation. No area, energy or realizable clock frequency was measured here.

## What could make a new contribution

A defensible claim would be a **specific** real, recurrent cue whose useful lifetime and retrieval trigger can be measured, a physical target family the issuer can actually express, and a simpler control that loses at equal budget and deadline. The most informative negative result would be that good retention can be implemented with PC separation, a sampled Markov record or a counter. Generalization across address placement and execution-time gain under competing traffic would materially strengthen a positive result. None of the papers above establishes those properties for these recordings and this LLC interface.
