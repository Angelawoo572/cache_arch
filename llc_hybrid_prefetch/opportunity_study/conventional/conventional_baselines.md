# What simple prefetchers already capture at the shared LLC

- A previous-delta table turned **3,646** MCF loads that missed with both NoPF and Stride into confirmed timely LLC hits in the MCF+CactuBSSN mix. Yet its 1.12% unconstrained completion-time gain over Stride reversed when both policies received exactly 28,949 accepted requests. Looking two strides ahead then won by 106,672 cycles. The architectural opportunity depends on *which* requests arrive earlier at a given traffic allowance. [Paired demand examples](conventional_examples.csv) · [Per-policy performance](policy_results.csv)
- A fixed conventional mechanism can win on the identical workload where it has lower timely accuracy. At 15,125 accepted requests in LBM+Xalancbmk, previous-delta saved 436,451 cycles over Stride, with 9,433 versus 12,718 timely uses. Its total DRAM traffic fell by 241,216 bytes. The number of timely fills alone gives a different ordering from completion time. [Equal-volume results](policy_results.csv)
- A target’s observed wait at the head of the reorder buffer was a weak discriminator of the actual *difference* between two small interventions here. Targeting 96 high-wait misses removed much of their recorded head wait, yet saved only 33 more mix cycles than targeting 96 zero-wait misses at matched PCs, lead times and pressures. [Criticality replay](criticality.csv)

## Measurement and comparisons

- Both programs run to their own fixed retirement counts on the same two-core Pythia v1.3 timing model. Solo tests keep the peer idle. Shared resources include a 4 MiB, 16-way LLC, 128 MSHRs, 64-entry request queues and one DRAM channel. Demand-only training observes admissions to the LLC read queue after the private levels, including hits in the LLC. Predictor histories warm before measurement. Issuance starts at the common retirement barrier. At most one address per admitted anchor can enter the native LLC prefetch queue.
- Original SPEC comparisons use 10M warmup and 5M measured instructions per core. Early GAP BFS uses 1M warmup and 2M measured. Later BFS warms at 49M–50M and measures at 50M–52M. The adjacent MCF region warms after skipping 5M instructions. Logical page allocation slots attach to programs, so a policy change preserves their physical placement. Degrees, machine resources, measured work and per-core input records match within each comparison. All percentages below express time saved as a fraction of the specified Stride time. The slower core determines a mix's completion time. [Workload manifest](../workloads/cases.json) · [Machine and event definitions](../../sim/README.md)
- The baseline condition reproduced both original NoPF and Stride outputs across 26 cases and 58 per-core comparisons. Per-core retirement cycles, placement counts, head waits, DRAM transfers and the other native totals all match exactly. Independent A–C regression checks were run before adding these isolated controls. [Validation](native_validation.csv) · [Source overlay](build.py)

## Per-core metrics for every new NoPF/Stride pair

- The 31 newly registered workloads supply 64 fixed-core/policy measurements, including both cores of BFS-8+LBM. Each core reports its own retirement IPC, demand L2/LLC tag hit and miss counts, rates and MPKI. Hit and miss fractions divide by completed demand tag lookups at that level; upstream forwards, coalesced requests without independent lookups and traces with zero LLC visibility remain distinguishable. MPKI divides by all retired instructions. [Per-core metrics](paired_metrics.csv)
- Proposed, accepted into LLC PQ, dispatched downstream and filled are separate counts. Accepted accuracy divides timely first hits plus first late demand merges by accepted requests. Timely accuracy uses first hits only. Timeliness conditions on a claimed prefetch. Every accepted request reconciles to exactly one terminal outcome: timely, late, unused eviction, redundant, or unresolved at ROI end. Lifecycle counts follow the issuer core. Coverage follows the matched demand core. Direct timely coverage divides proven first-use hits on original NoPF misses by all NoPF LLC tag misses. Direct inclusive coverage adds original misses that first merged into an outstanding prefetch. A late merge need not remove the demand miss. The observed issue degree divides accepted requests by LLC demand ingresses. An empty ratio marks a zero denominator and integer zero records an observed count. [Derivation and checks](paired_metrics.py)
- Early BFS-8 has the same 25,373 LLC ingresses and 24,569 accepted prefetches alone and with LBM. Of 24,568 requests with the same anchor, consumer and physical target, 5,820 were timely alone and late with LBM. In this subgroup median proposal-to-demand lead is 172 vs 174 cycles. Queue-to-dispatch medians are both 20, while dispatch-to-fill rises from 70 to 233 and fill-to-demand slack shifts from +80 to −88 cycles. The matched lifecycles isolate where the delay occurred on these traces. [Service intervals](bfs_service.csv)

| Policy | History and action at one admitted demand |
|---|---|
| Native Stride, look2, look4, next | The release tracks 64 PCs per core and requires two equal consecutive nonzero line deltas. The lookahead arms issue only the second or fourth stride. Next-line tries one line ahead on every anchor. |
| Multidelta, boundary | A 256-entry table indexed by requesting PC and its preceding delta predicts the next delta after two observations. A separate boundary control records the previous length of a constant-delta run of at least three and its departing jump, and targets the next run up to two strides early. Both retain Stride when their own state is silent. |
| Cross-PC, temporal | A 256-entry table maps previous PC, current PC and current line offset to next delta. A separate 256-entry table maps an exact observed physical line to its previously observed successor. Both require repeated support. Cross-PC uses a same-page issue filter. The temporal test varies whether a stored physical successor can cross a page. |
| SPP | The Pythia signature and pattern helpers supply per-page delta signatures, a 256-entry signature table, a 512-set four-way pattern table and global feedback. The LLC port caps lookahead at four stages and selects the highest-confidence same-page candidate before ordinary queue checks. This evaluates a specific degree-one SPP configuration. |
| Union, confirm, priority, pressure, confidence | Union gives native Stride first choice, followed by same-page cross-PC, then an observed physical successor. Confirm requires another table to produce exactly the native address. Priority applies that requirement only at LLC MSHR or DRAM read-queue occupancy ≥16. Pressure suppresses native requests at that threshold. Confidence waits for a same-PC run of four equal deltas. |
| Promote | The same union selection finds confirmations of earlier pending Stride candidates. An already ready prefetch-queue entry can advance to the head. This changes scheduling only when a legal slot exists. No duplicate is issued. |

- The small conventional tables use direct mapping, full tags and two-bit saturation. A mismatch reduces confidence, then replaces an unsupported prediction. The temporal table learns from completed *observations of arrivals*. It issues only a physical target it has already seen in the core's own address space. The remaining delta mechanisms stay on the current physical page. These experiments use no future target, hit, or head-wait features. The explicitly marked intervention below uses future labels for diagnosis. - The budgeted comparisons use two controls. The paced control permits up to eight new issues per core per 512 retired instructions, resetting credits each epoch. MCF second-region Stride offered so few requests that the common volume is 104, making its unrestricted SPP gain largely inaccessible under this control. [Achieved caps by core](policy_by_core.csv)

## What the synthetic controls establish

- **Repeated row lengths.** On alternating 8/24-line rows, cross-PC/offset saves 2.474%, multidelta 2.000%, and the fixed last-run-length boundary mechanism *loses* 1.405% against Stride. Multidelta supplies 1,023 attributable timely hits that missed under both baselines. Its count includes body loads and row entries. On the five-length case union saves 1.996%. A previous-length control or phase label at a row boundary needs target-specific evaluation beyond total runtime.
- **Three-level rows and compiled rows.** Union saves 2.043% on changing slab/band/subrow lengths. On the bounded x86 program compiled from C, cross-PC/offset saves 4.413% and multidelta saves 3.589%. Synthetic pacing and controlled addresses produce distinct memory-system behavior from compiled execution. [Program and ground-truth definitions](../workloads/README.md)
- **Interleaving.** With two alternating streams sharing one PC, native Stride issues zero. Same-page SPP makes 8,064 verified timely hits to loads that missed under both baselines and cuts time by 18.497%. Giving the two streams separate PCs reduces the SPP advantage over Stride to 0.771%. On three streams with alternating scheduling order, SPP saves 20.434% when PCs are shared. With separate PCs, the best gain drops to 1.493% from multidelta.
- **Separated cues and irregular motifs.** The one-cue stimulus and its masked version receive exactly the same 4.655% reduction from multidelta. SPP likewise yields 8.142% on both the two-cue XOR stimulus and its first-cue-masked control. Other events in these traces are predictable. Identical whole-run results in the controls do not establish use of the cue. The independent motif plus unrelated accesses already yields a 12.426% SPP gain. A candidate memory mechanism must show *which intended targets* improve beyond these controls. [All synthetic results](policy_results.csv) · [Cue visibility measurements](../workloads/cue_visibility.csv)
- **Observed cross-page reuse.** A directed single-program fixture touches 19 pages in a fixed shuffled order after priming page placement and forcing same-set conflicts. The same-page table rejects 1,632 cross-page proposals and accepts zero. The identical table allowed to reuse previously seen physical targets accepts 592, records 591 timely hits and cuts time from 909,448 to 876,198 cycles. Total DRAM traffic increases from 24,576 to 51,264 bytes. None of the tested SPEC or BFS arms issued a cross-page temporal candidate with this 256-entry table. This establishes the hardware path and a workload-specific cost. [Cross-page fixture](temporal_fixture.csv)

## Issue timing, pressure and confirmation

- An 8- or 32-cycle observation-to-ready delay uses a shared, pipelined decision unit with initiation interval one and 16 pending candidates. After readiness, an address enters the real tag/MSHR/request/PQ checks. A later demand can already own the request by then. The asynchronous union test sends native proposals directly and charges that delay only to added proposals. Decision latency is a modeled sensitivity; it has no clock-frequency or area equivalence.
- On MCF+CactuBSSN a 32-cycle delay applied to the whole union costs 41,534 cycles against its zero-delay reference. Preserving the native fast path reduces this loss to 378 cycles. On LBM+Xalancbmk the synchronous 32-cycle arm finishes in 15,494,977 cycles, while the fast-native variant takes 15,509,180. Later BFS-3 sees a 1,847-cycle penalty with the synchronous path and a 65-cycle penalty with the fast-native path. Arbitration and the time at which an address becomes redundant can change which candidates reach DRAM. [Decision-latency comparisons](policy_results.csv)
- Strong controls also run through the delayed pipeline. In later BFS-3, charging 32 cycles to SPP changes completion time from 12,005,882 to 12,033,460, still well ahead of 13,800,720 for equally delayed Stride. On LBM+Xalancbmk, delayed multidelta takes 14,400,438 cycles versus 15,531,088 for delayed Stride. [Strong-control timing](policy_results.csv)
- Native Stride on MCF+CactuBSSN produces 36,793 proposals, of which 6,007 already reside in LLC, 116 hit a live MSHR, 25 are already in the PQ and 1,223 cross the page boundary. It accepts 29,422. The admission pressure flag activates on 807 out of 180,419 anchors. On LBM+Xalancbmk it activates on 19,650 out of 137,499 anchors. On MCF region 2 it never activates. Because the guard reports the first failing check, rejected categories are mutually exclusive. An inactive gate in a low-pressure region supplies no evidence about the heavy-load regime. [Resource counts](resources.csv)
- At low pressure and a same-PC stride run length of at least four, corroborated MCF+CactuBSSN Stride predictions are timely in 233/713 accepted cases (32.7%). Uncorroborated predictions in the same coarse bin are timely in 9,718/18,622 cases (52.2%). On LBM+Xalancbmk none of the 16,824 native acceptances have contemporaneous agreement. The confirm-only policy eliminates prefetching in that mix and matches NoPF. Unrestricted pressure-gated union saves 0.081% on MCF+CactuBSSN and 0.025% on LBM+Xalancbmk. Sparse or selection-biased agreement makes a blanket confirmation rule unattractive here. [Conditioned outcomes](agreement.csv)
- The promotion experiment separately asks if a later source confirming the *same pending physical address* can advance an existing ready PQ entry. It observes 309 confirmations in MCF+CactuBSSN (297 resident, 10 already in MSHR and two already at queue head). LBM+Xalancbmk has 1,718 (1,515 resident, 183 in MSHR, 13 already at head, seven waiting for readiness). Neither mix had a ready non-head candidate to promote. Promotion reproduced ordinary union timing in every tested case. This argues for basic physical duplicate checks and logging actual scheduling opportunities before building a learned arbiter. [Promotion counts](resources.csv)
- The directed shared-address test sends core 0 and core 1 issuer requests to the exact same physical line. Core 0 is accepted at cycle 533. Core 1 is rejected in the PQ at 533, by the MSHR at 554 and by LLC residency at 744. The test uses the real shared cache queue and outstanding-request machinery. Native mixed processes receive distinct page sets; the fixture supplies cross-core physical aliases at the issuer and does not exercise cross-thread coherence, shared demand ownership transfer or data sharing. [One-line fixture](duplicate_fixture.csv)
- With multidelta on MCF+CactuBSSN, 2,017 paired demands switch from NoPF hits to policy misses. For 549, a prefetch fill is the last observed evictor of the needed physical line, including 20 Cactus misses after an MCF fill. For SPP, that association holds for 768 of 2,295 total reversals, including 170 cross-core cases. Multidelta on LBM+Xalancbmk associates 66 of 170 reversals with a prefetch eviction; 64 are cross-core. These are same-line event sequences. Competing demand replacements and scheduling changes can also affect the counterfactual; one such count is not a proved harmful transaction or an additive cycle cost. [Verified victim replays](pollution.csv)

## Which earlier requests reduce completion time?

- In MCF+CactuBSSN, 25,428 surviving MCF demand misses had a different previously admitted same-page anchor within one to three measured median service times and after the previous reference to the target. The median dispatch-to-fill interval was 190 cycles. The high group starts at the 75th percentile among positive waits (120 cycles). Greedy selection from largest wait downward matched 96 high targets to 96 zero-wait targets using target PC, binned lead, delta sign, near/far offset and binary anchor pressure. Median observed baseline leads were 414 and 414.5 cycles. Groups use disjoint anchor and target identities. [Selection and paired replay](critical.py)
- Both groups override one decision slot at each selected anchor. In the high-wait set ordinary Stride had been silent at 25 anchors, accepted at 47 and rejected at 24. In the zero-wait set these counts were 12, 71 and 13. All 96 planned anchors survived and all 96 requests were accepted in each replay. The groups have different displaced native traffic. All share the original same-page and queue path. The high group makes 93 intended accesses timely and three late; the zero group makes 95 timely and one late. Median ready-to-dispatch is 20 in both groups. Median dispatch-to-fill is 89 versus 111.5; median fill-to-target-ingress slack is 210 versus 252 cycles. Values refer to events within their own policy run.
- High-target mean head wait falls from 510.56 to 42.01 cycles. The zero group remains at zero. The actual mix saves 2,205 versus 2,172 cycles; CactuBSSN costs increase by 4,623 versus 8,043 cycles. Combined DRAM traffic changes by +320 versus −256 bytes; total accepts change by +36 versus −35 after indirect changes to the stream. These deterministic two-group results do not estimate a population effect or the isolated savings of each individual prefetch. The benefit of a correlated online early-head signal still requires a prospective test across windows. [Intervention totals](criticality.csv)
- MCF target 10,600,934 illustrates the difference between removed head wait and saved execution time. In the high-group replay, anchor 10,600,844 selects physical line 376998 five lines ahead; ready/accept, dispatch, fill and target LLC ingress occur at 11,976,609, 11,976,629, 11,976,820 and 11,977,200. Its original 1,356 head-wait cycles fall to zero. Only a full-run comparison assigns a performance change. Future targets and baseline head waits are supplied here solely by the diagnostic plan. ## Hardware implications and reproduction

- With illustrative 48-bit byte addresses and 48-bit PCs, lines require 42 bits and a full-width signed difference needs 43. A 256-entry tagged PC/previous-delta table needs about 4.28 KiB per core (48+43+43+2+1 bits per entry), a previous-PC/current-PC/offset table about 4.63 KiB, and an exact physical-successor table about 2.72 KiB. These counts exclude native predictor state, the small per-PC run tracker, replacement or queue metadata, tag comparators and routing. The C++ structures use full host integers. The SPP control has a separate 1,024-entry direct-mapped feedback structure and 256-way signature search. It has different storage and lookup requirements from the smaller controls.
- Hash, tagged table read/write, signed addition and parallel duplicate checks are conventional logic. A new SRAM read port, a multi-stage SPP lookup, and a shared arbitration port have real timing and energy costs. No RTL, synthesis or SRAM macro measurement was made. The 0/8/32-cycle experiments only show workload sensitivity and queue interactions. A future implementation needs a defined process, clock, bank/port organization and throughput target before reporting timing, area or energy.
- The controlled comparisons support a simple two-stride lookahead candidate, a prior-delta table and an SPP control in the next round. Confirmation alone has limited evidence; the directed duplicate hazard is already handled by cache hardware. The remaining question is whether earlier, nonadjacent observations add *target-specific timely coverage and net cycle savings* over these competitors at a fixed traffic budget. The fixed-program criticality interventions show how to test such a claim without treating future head wait as an online feature.
