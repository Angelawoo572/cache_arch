# Full-rate conventional controls on LBM R2

On 619.lbm_s-3766B, a 256-entry PC/previous-delta table reduced execution time more than the sparse current-PC/current-offset rule, both at its native issue rate and under a common request ceiling. Its open configuration saved **1,240,373 cycles (16.40%)** over Stride. With exactly the original Stride arm’s 12,276 accepted prefetches, it saved **485,281 cycles (6.42%)**. The earlier current-PC/current-offset hybrid saved 269,697 cycles (3.57%) with 13,493 acceptances. This is one recording examined after the page-history finding. - The common ceiling is the archived Stride acceptance count, chosen before the strong-control runs. When it bound, the issuer rejected 13,380 otherwise eligible multidelta requests and 14,355 SPP requests on budget. This whole-ROI cap lets a predictor consume the budget early. The open multidelta arm moved 1,641,792 prefetch-read bytes and 5,431,232 demand-read bytes, versus 785,600 and 6,288,000 for Stride. Total transferred bytes alone obscure that change. The sparse hybrid keyed its exact-context table on the current PC’s low 12 bits and exact current line offset with backoff. It retained ordinary Stride on nongranted ingresses, with one opportunity to replace or agree per eight ingresses. The native controls used their candidate at each eligible demand ingress. [Registered budgets and settings](protocol.md) · [Traffic by source](results.csv)
- The 5,429 distinct dynamic operands that broke a timely-hit run in at least one original projection were held fixed. The sparse current-PC/current-offset rule (lbm_exact) directly converted 1,834. The separate rule including the previous offset on the same physical page (lbm_page) directly converted 2,179. Open SPP converted 3,459, of which 1,646 were also directly served by lbm_exact. Open multidelta converted 1,802, of which 886 overlapped. Thus 948 lbm_exact first uses were absent from open multidelta’s direct conversions, even though multidelta finished much earlier. The history-bearing lbm_page rule shared 1,976 of its 2,179 conversions with SPP and 1,037 with multidelta. These are overlap counts from independent replays. They leave the cost and timing of jointly issuing these candidates unmeasured. Each claim was checked against the original operand’s physical target. [Same-operand first-use comparisons](endpoints.csv)
- Across all four open conventional arms, the union of their direct timely first uses covered 3,654 of 5,429 original failure operands. Outside that union, lbm_exact directly converted 96 (85 auxiliary, 11 native on altered timing); lbm_page converted 105 (96 auxiliary, 9 native). These sets overlap on 87, leaving 114 distinct operands. **SPP predicted all 114 addresses and arrived late on every one.** Multidelta was late on 102 and had no observed target proposal before the other 12 lookups. Cross-PC was late on 17; the physical-successor control missed all 114 without a target proposal. Every survivor missed at LLC tag in every strong arm. This evidence isolates a delivery advantage on particular loads. Any claim about combined execution time still requires a live shared-issuer experiment. [Union and attribution](union_results.csv) · [All targets with competitor outcomes and causal timestamps](uncovered_targets.csv)
- **An earlier producer explains the delivery advantage in the selected 85 lbm_exact auxiliary first uses.** The exact-offset rule used an earlier dynamic operand than SPP in 84 cases and the same operand once. Program order and producer rank in the original Stride ingress stream agreed in all 85. Median proposal-to-demand-ingress lead was 1,384 cycles for lbm_exact and 266 for SPP; the median within-operand difference was +1,130 cycles, with greater lead in all 85. Issue-to-dispatch cost 20 cycles in both arms for every pair. Dispatch-to-fill service medians were 312 and 346 cycles; paired service differences had median +3 cycles, with lbm_exact slower on 43 and faster on 42. Median fill slack at demand ingress was +1,087 versus −101 cycles. These durations are measured against the consumer in each policy’s own run. This cohort was selected for a delivery difference and does not estimate its prevalence elsewhere. [Per-operand producer identities and timestamps](timing_targets.csv) · [Distributions and paired differences](timing_summary.csv)
- **Two named operands show how budgets change the result.** Stride missed instruction 12,657,217, physical line 552063, with 333 operand head-wait cycles. Open SPP delivered a timely hit (10 head-wait cycles), while open multidelta arrived late (244). Both missed under the common acceptance cap. Stride also missed instruction 10,399,777, line 451157, with 586 head-wait cycles. Both open and capped SPP delivered a timely hit (13). Both multidelta arms delivered a late merge (28). Cross-PC and temporal missed both operands in both settings. Hardware timing can distinguish policies that correctly infer the same target. [Per-arm demand, candidate, dispatch and fill events](witness.csv)
- The simulator used one active core in the original two-core geometry, the archived physical-page slot and trace, 10M warmup and 5M measured instructions. Each arm checked its complete virtual-to-physical page map against the archived Stride run. Both Stride replays reproduced its retirement and every original stats counter exactly. The standalone temporal arm admitted zero requests. The same-page multidelta policy falls back to native Stride when its trained table does not return a prediction. SPP uses the archived same-page degree-one port; these results describe this implementation and recording.
