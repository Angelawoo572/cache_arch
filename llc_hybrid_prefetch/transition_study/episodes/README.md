# What interrupts a run of timely Stride hits?

A run of hits at the shared LLC looks different to the core, to the Stride predictor and to a single load PC. In MCF 665B with CactuBSSN, two or more consecutive attributable timely hits immediately preceded 71 paired misses in the LLC ingress stream. Restricting that stream to one PC at a time exposed 2,537 such misses. The complete core load stream, which retains the loads absorbed upstream, had zero. In early BFS-8 one PC dominated the LLC, so the per-PC and combined ingress counts agreed. Sharing the machine with LBM changed which of those requests arrived on time. ## Which failures recur?

The table uses the per-core LLC arrival stream. “Late” and “persistent without a late claim” are mutually exclusive types of a paired LLC miss. The denominator is the number of occasions on which the last two or more projected events were attributable timely hits and another projected event followed. - **A missed service deadline can interrupt an unchanged pattern.** All 516 late BFS-8 solo breaks followed a native target equal to the next demand, with unchanged delta and page. On the matched continued-hit controls, mean ingress separation was 241 cycles versus 89 for these failures. Mean DRAM read-queue occupancy at the target ingress was 0.03 versus 0.95. With LBM, 2,447 of the 2,742 late breaks had been directly timely on those exact same dynamic operands in the solo run. The data show a change in delivery under shared service. Interarrival spacing and queue occupancy covary, so their individual causal contributions cannot be identified from this comparison. [Matched comparison](comparison.csv) · [Prior target actions](reasons.csv)
- **A correct target and an address transition require different responses in MCF.** In MCF+CactuBSSN core 0's per-PC ingress view, 1,624 breaks were late and 913 were other persistent misses. Among the latter, 761 target lines had no recorded action since the previous same-line LLC lookup, 151 last saw a page rejection and one last saw an eviction. In the matched persistent subset, the physical-line delta changed for 76.3% of failures and 0.2% of continued hits. Among matched late breaks the previous trigger named the failing address 97.7% of the time. Only 447 of all 1,624 late-break anchors had completed their own LLC tag lookup strictly before the next ingress. A controller using a completed streak as its issue-time signal would miss most of them. [Episode identities and event times](episode_ids.csv.gz) · [Matched event features](comparison.csv)
- **A source-level row boundary is independently checkable.** In the compiled loop, 74 observed runs of at least two direct hits terminated at a row-start miss. On the first example, Stride converted lines 757, 758 and 759, missed row entry line 768 at instruction 43,116, and recovered by line 771 at instruction 43,518. The known 8/24 and 5-length synthetic controls also produced 511 observed terminations each. In the three switched streams the shared-PC variant had zero attributable timely conversions and therefore zero such episodes; distinct stream PCs produced 256 qualifying breaks. The episode method selects intervals after successes and cannot discover all-silent patterns. CactuBSSN 3477B had 38,457 observed LLC ingresses but zero direct timely conversions. [Source-label counts](synthetic_labels.csv) · [Exact example timelines](timeline.csv)
- **An LLC boundary alone does not identify a loop or even a PC change.** Later BFS-3 had 2,700 global-ingress persistent breaks; 2,694 had no recorded action on the target line and six last saw page rejection. Among 632 matched persistent breaks, 96.5% changed PC and 94.5% changed physical page. Among their continuations, 81.3% changed PC and 64.6% changed page. Delta changed nearly always in both subsets. These rates describe confusable pattern changes and carry substantial matching exclusions. LBM 4268B alone had 4,308 ingress breaks but only 82 eligible matched controls, so matched averages there cannot characterize the whole LBM population. ## How each operand and run was identified

1. Read every `CORE/trace` load operand under NoPF and Stride, verify exact equality of core, original instruction ordinal, operand slot, load PC and virtual line, then attach each policy's cache outcome. The measured sequence includes original loads served or merged before the LLC. `path_pairs.csv` distinguishes L1/L2 hits, private merges/forwards, LLC RQ merges or forwards, LLC tag hits/misses and unresolved paths with no recorded lookup. A physical frame for a private-cache hit comes from the validated per-core page map. This is an analyst's projection of all operands. The LLC only observes a subset. No policies are aligned by cycle or by demand-miss sequence.
2. A **T** is a NoPF LLC lookup miss that becomes a Stride LLC lookup hit and claims the first demand use of a timely Stride prefetch. **L** is a miss in both policies with a confirmed merge into an outstanding Stride prefetch. **P** is a miss in both with no such claim. Any other event interrupts a T run, including an ordinary LLC hit, an unattributed NoPF-miss-to-hit conversion, or an upper-cache event in a core stream. A NoPF-hit-to-Stride-miss has its own outcome. The exact NoPF/Stride paths remain attached for every other outcome. [All event classes](population.csv)
3. Form four program-order projections per core (entire stream, each exact PC, each physical 4 KiB page and each aligned four-page physical region). Form the same four projections in Stride LLC demand-ingress order, independent of program order. No runs span different cores, group keys or ROI ends. Events belonging to other projection keys are skipped only in that keyed view. The raw core-position and ingress-position differences preserve the skipped gap. Signed differences can be negative when service and program order disagree. Intervening events remain available in the aligned source arrays.
4. A maximal run of length one or greater contributes to `endings.csv`, including interruptions by ordinary hits and upper paths. The detailed break cohort requires at least two Ts followed immediately in that projection by L or P. Recovery is the next T on the same stream. The recovery gap counts intervening non-T events **including the first failure**. An unavailable return is right-censored with blank IDs. The start is left-censored at the beginning of its projected stream. An ending at the stream's final event has no following outcome. Each view's denominator includes only anchors with an observed successor. Projections overlap and their counts must be read separately.
5. Match a failure anchor to one continued-T anchor without replacement within case, core and view, at the same last-T PC, physical offset quartile, quarter of core program progress, and consecutive-T prefix length capped at eight. Select the nearest available anchor in core operand position, breaking ties toward the earlier position. Page-keyed comparisons can match a different physical page; the row records whether the group key agrees. Prefix length ≥8 and page offset quartiles group unequal exact values. This is a descriptive comparison with explicit support; a control does not establish causal performance savings. [Matched identities](matched_ids.csv.gz)
6. The last recorded target action is sought at a strictly earlier cycle and after the previous same-line demand lookup on that core. Track candidate proposal, admission or rejection, dispatch, fill, eviction and other lifecycle outcomes. The anchor's own tracker/admission state describes what it predicted **from** that access. A target's own tracker state is only known once that target arrives. A missing prior action can reflect multiple causes. Retrospective labels T/L/P, ultimate first use and operand retirement-head wait are unavailable at trigger time. A strict tag-before-next-ingress comparison allows an optimistic screening of whether the preceding last hit could already have been known. It excludes same-cycle events; there can also be additional feedback transport latency. Operand head waits can overlap and cannot be summed into processor cycles saved.

## Reproduction and validation

From this directory use Python with NumPy and pandas. The archived run files and per-core ingress extracts are prerequisites. The two middle-window caches and raw logs have their own paths in the transition study. The extractor uses the existing history extractor if a new case has raw runs but no ingress cache.

```sh
python3 -B extract.py --jobs 4
python3 -B report.py --jobs 4
python3 -B verify.py
```

`verify.py` independently reconstructs eligible last-T indices in every projection, compares all endpoint identities, recovers uninterrupted run lengths, verifies the next T for every observed recovery and checks every matched stable control for continuity and reuse. The compact index contains exact searchable IDs. The fuller arrays in the transition study's raw episode directory have one row per program-order operand and an explicit ingress index. The initial 16 archive pairs and two fresh BFS midpoint pairs comprise 21 per-core sequences and 168 projection populations. Those original populations have 99,951 endpoint rows and 47,802 matched controls.

The later LBM R2 supplement adds one recording, eight projections, 12,834 endpoint rows and 1,610 matched controls. On per-core LLC ingress, 4,020 of its 4,135 streak anchors ended in a paired miss without a late claim; 115 had eligible stable controls. Across all 19 case pairs, the independent check covered 112,785 overlapping endpoint rows and 49,412 matched controls. LBM R2 was added after a forecast result motivated a native comparison. It is a separate mechanism check on an already inspected recording. [Case roster and registration groups](case_roster.csv)
