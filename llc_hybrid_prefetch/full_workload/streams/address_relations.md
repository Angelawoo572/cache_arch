# Can one stream supply the address increment of another?

The motivating observations are the previously published interleaved accesses in the project and Voyager's analysis of related sparse-solver arrays. Earlier experiments tested associations between a tuple of producer PCs and a fixed displacement. Here the displacement can change at every observation. This tests a different possible relationship: two interleaved loads may move through different arrays according to similar indices. A positive result could also justify two small stride trackers. [Voyager, Figure 16](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~lin/papers/asplos21.pdf)

Before examining the new diagnostic results, fix three degree-one address proposals at a Stride demand ingress. The local control takes the current line plus its own last signed line increment. The transfer control adds the most recent other PC's last observed increment to the current line. The continuation control advances that other PC's last line by its own increment. The latter tests whether merely revisiting an interrupted stream explains an apparent cross-PC opportunity. The other PC must have appeared among the last eight ingresses. A 64-entry direct-mapped tagged table retains the last line and last increment. Each candidate must stay on the already-observed physical page of its base, differ from the trigger line, and have a nonzero increment. No thresholds are fitted, no PC/target dictionary is imported, and no issue decision uses a future label.

For each issued *offline proposal*, find the first subsequent demand ingress for the line among the next 16 Stride ingresses of the same logging island. Give credit only for a matched dynamic operand that missed at the NoPF LLC. Use a 130-cycle screening threshold frozen on earlier donor data. Also report the actual lead distribution. The service threshold is a proxy for useful lead. Exclude an island's last 16 anchors, reset tracker state at its beginning, and compare on the same anchors. Count unique dynamic targets, including those covered by another rule, those without an attributed timely first use under any of the four prefetching controls, those with a measured tag miss in all four, and lines unseen earlier in that island. Sampling selects instruction ordinals, so at the edges we additionally discard contexts near the first/last logged ingress; observations represent the logged island. Look for displacement sharing that transfers across programs, is absent from the local control, and remains physically issuable. A high hit count for a subset of offered addresses cannot by itself establish marginal performance.

The first exploration of BFS-3 produced a +1 example. This prompted an explicit diagnostic control before evaluating the rest of the panel. Six degree-one fixed actions use the current physical line plus ±1, ±2 or ±4 under the same page, horizon and lead constraints. The final column of the result table removes targets reached by **any** of these six offline actions. Their union bounds what this particular candidate vocabulary can reach; an online issuer would have to pick an action without seeing the target.

The BFS-3 first island contained transfers beyond the six fixed actions. Prospectively test the mechanism on BFS-14, Soplex and GAP PageRank as well as that discovery source. In each recording retire the original 50M warmup and next 1M measured instructions from the start. These secondary windows overlap the main panel and restore ordinary simulator operation for the selected requests. Compare NoPF, Stride, Multi-delta, SPP, matched fixed +1 and +3, and the three arithmetic alternatives. Each alternative can replace one Stride offer every eight ingresses (slot 3 modulo 8), otherwise Stride proceeds. Checked duplicates or illegal addresses cannot issue two requests. Track agreement, displaced offers, directly timely original misses gained and lost, and cycles. Fixed +3 is a diagnostic control motivated by the BFS discovery result. Full-window and whole-recording marginal effects for the other mechanism are in the discovery report.

The additional arithmetic table, eight-PC history, and sparse grant counter start empty at the measurement boundary. The processor, caches and conventional predictor received the 50M warmup. The same auxiliary reset applies to all five replacement alternatives.

Each percentage compares the same first 1M measured instructions after 50M warmup. All four workloads gained more original direct hits than they lost under shared increments; the two independent-program windows nonetheless took longer. The same-PC alternative, which requires no other-PC lookup, completed earlier than the cross-PC increment in every row. The BFS result motivates comparing related streams; extracting their last increments alone did not beat the lower-cost control here.
