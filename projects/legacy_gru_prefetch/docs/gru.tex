\begin{frame}{Where this deck starts}
  \textbf{V1--V4} (last deck): controlled-variable sweep on \texttt{mcf $\to$ omnetpp} cross-binary.
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item All variants: IPC --3\% to --12\%, trigger rate 58\%, val/test acc gap 3$\times$.
    \item Diagnosis: 64-class offset prediction is near-chance on pointer-chase.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{V8} (between decks): redesign with delta-target output, top-256 delta vocab, L1DM filter, confidence gate.
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item test top-1 = 47.9\% (was 1.3\% in V4) -- \textbf{36$\times$ accuracy improvement}.
    \item BUT IPC = 0.9604$\times$ -- \textbf{essentially unchanged from V4 (0.889$\times$)}.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \textbf{This deck} asks: why did 36$\times$ accuracy improvement not translate to IPC?
  And what does the answer tell us to do next (V9).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The 5 insights from V8 (each citable)}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High ML accuracy $\neq$ high IPC.} V8 47.9\% top-1 vs V1 1.3\% top-1, same IPC.
      \emph{Princeton MEMSYS 2023 already showed this; this run confirms it on our trace pair.}

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \item \textbf{omnetpp is a bad showcase trace.} From the slide-8 upper bound:
      LRU 0.2806, +IP-stride 0.2822, +SPP 0.2833, SRRIP+SPP 0.2786 --
      total spread is \textbf{0.4\%}. Idealized prefetcher only gains 1\%. No NN can show big IPC here.

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \item \textbf{Delta distribution is heavy-tailed.} top-256 covers 6.6\% of unique deltas
      but 33.3\% of label occurrences. The other 66.7\% of labels are tail entries that no
      finite vocab can capture. Need either a much larger vocab or a different output space.

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \item \textbf{The model's natural OOV-prediction is already a gate.} 65\% of accesses,
      the model predicts OOV ("I don't know"). Adding a softmax confidence threshold on top
      only filters another 1\%. Real gating must work at the bitmap-position level.

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \item \textbf{Cross-binary is the wrong eval.} val top-1 = 0.746 (mcf $\to$ mcf),
      test top-1 = 0.479 (mcf $\to$ omnetpp). PC embeddings don't transfer.
      \emph{Voyager paper: "a trained model with one application execution cannot be used by another."}
      \emph{Pythia retrains per program. Hashemi 2018 trained per application.} We did it wrong.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{V9 redesign -- three concrete changes}
  \textbf{Each change has a paper-cited motivation; we can ablate independently.}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{center}
  \scriptsize
  \begin{tabular}{p{1.0cm}p{4.4cm}p{3.6cm}p{4.0cm}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Tag} & \textbf{Change} & \textbf{Source} & \textbf{Predicted effect} \\
    \midrule
    A & \textbf{In-distribution split}: train[0:70\%], val[70:85\%], test[85:100\%] on the \emph{same} trace. & Voyager ASPLOS'21; Pythia MICRO'21; Hashemi ICML'18 all train per-application. & Removes 0.27 generalization gap V8 observed. \\

    B & \textbf{Delta-bitmap output} (multi-label): predict which of 129 nearby deltas appear in next 8 accesses. BCE loss, sigmoid head, variable prefetch degree. & TransFetch CF'22: address segmentation $+$ delta bitmaps. DART arXiv 2401.06362 uses same. & Handles long-tail (no fixed vocab on output side); natural variable degree. \\

    C & \textbf{PC+Delta hash} feature: hash(PC $\oplus$ last\_delta) embedding, added to the GRU output. & Pythia MICRO'21, \S 4: PC+Delta + last-4-deltas is the winning state vector for RL prefetcher. & Captures PC--delta interaction the GRU can't learn from delta history alone. \\
    \midrule
    D & Optional: \textbf{train per trace} and report IPC on traces where prefetching actually matters: \texttt{lbm} (+18\% from SPP), \texttt{gcc} (+139\% from SPP). & slide-8 sensitivity. & Real IPC headroom to demonstrate gains. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \footnotesize
  Architecture: GRU(hidden=96, layers=1) on delta-token history (vocab=2048+OOV),
  with PC and PC+Delta embeddings concatenated to the final hidden state,
  feeding a 2-layer MLP head to a 129-bit sigmoid bitmap. BCE loss with
  pos\_weight=avg-inverse-density. AdamW, cosine LR, gradient clip.
\end{frame}

    \texttt{605.mcf\_s-994B}      & pointer-chase    & 0.439  & 0.430  & \textbf{$-$2.1\%} \\

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \textbf{Drop \texttt{omnetpp} as the demo trace.} 1\% headroom means even a perfect prefetcher
  can only get 1\% IPC. V8's $-$4\% is bad but not surprising.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{New trace order for V9} (cheapest first):
  \begin{enumerate}\scriptsize
    \item \texttt{605.mcf\_s} in-distribution -- same trace as before, direct V1..V8 comparison.
    \item \texttt{619.lbm\_s} in-distribution -- streaming, easy for NN, +18\% IPC headroom.
    \item \texttt{602.gcc\_s} in-distribution -- branch, +139\% IPC headroom (most ambitious).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}
