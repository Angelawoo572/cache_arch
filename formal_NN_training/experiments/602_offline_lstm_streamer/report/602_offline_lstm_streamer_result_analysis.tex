\section*{Status and experiment contract}
\textbf{Status: PASS.} This is a single-trace offline experiment. Colab trains a one-layer LSTM on an earlier no-prefetch training prefix and performs causal sequential inference on a disjoint no-prefetch evaluation stream. It exports keyed PC--line--occurrence replay lists. ChampSim replays both the deterministic offline-streamer list and each offline-LSTM list using the same \texttt{ListReplayer} transport.

The matched normal prefetcher is the pinned implementation of streamer with 64 page trackers and degree 5. Its raw input is the current cache-line address plus causal prior address history. PC is retained only in the replay key; it is not an LSTM input. The LSTM may derive causal page/offset/direction state from that same address stream and only gates the exact streamer candidate set. It receives no hit/miss, cycle, queue, metadata, or future evaluation information.

The primary comparison is \texttt{offline\_streamer} versus \texttt{offline\_lstm\_h<width>}. Live streamer is a validated general reference only. All simulations used the same 25M warmup and 25M measured window (25,000,003 reported instructions). The provenance analyzer passed for every capacity using canonical decompressed-content SHA256 checks.

\section*{Accuracy, coverage, and timeliness diagnosis}
The event log does not expose a strict prefetch-accuracy counter. We use \emph{observed useful-hit yield}---useful-prefetch demand hits divided by post-warmup prefetch-request events---as a diagnostic proxy. It is not strict accuracy because requests may be rejected, merged, duplicated, or evicted before use. Coverage is useful hits divided by the 203,131 no-prefetch L2 demand misses. Late/request is a timeliness proxy, not a complete lead-time distribution.

Offline streamer has an observed useful-hit yield of 11.562\%, coverage of 92.374\%, and 48 late misses (0.00296\% of requests). The closest model, h8, has nearly the same yield (11.556\%) and slightly lower coverage (92.257\%). It removes 1,169 requests but loses 239 useful hits; the same 239 reappear as additional L2 demand misses. It reduces late misses by only six. This is the specific measured reason h8 does not reach the offline-streamer line: its small timeliness improvement does not compensate for lost useful coverage.

The larger h32--h128 gates illustrate a different trade-off. Their useful-hit yield rises to 13.95--15.51\%, so retained requests are more selective by this proxy, and late misses fall to one or two. However, they remove 19--28\% of requests, lose 2.25--3.38\% of useful hits, and reduce coverage to 90.29--89.25\%. Their higher yield is therefore purchased by excessive coverage loss, and IPC falls. Accuracy-like yield alone is not the objective; useful coverage and timing must be preserved together.

\section*{What the data supports}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No LSTM win in this experiment:} h8 is the best LSTM point at 545 parameters and IPC 0.43134, but it is 0.00016 IPC (0.037\%) below offline streamer. Every other tested width is lower. Therefore there is no streamer-matched LSTM capacity to select or confirm as a winner.
\item \textbf{The primary control is sound:} offline streamer is 0.43150, only 0.00011 below active live streamer (0.43161). The two methods also have essentially equal useful hits, coverage, and L2 miss reduction. The offline replay baseline is therefore a faithful counterpart of the live normal prefetcher.
\item \textbf{Why h8 is close but loses:} relative to offline streamer, h8 drops only 1,169 request events (0.072\%) and 153 matched trigger keys, but loses 239 useful-prefetch demand hits. Its L2 demand misses increase by exactly 239 (15,736 versus 15,497), producing the small IPC loss. Its six fewer late misses do not compensate for that coverage loss.
\item \textbf{Increasing capacity is harmful here:} h32--h128 suppress 19--28\% of the streamer's request events, reduce useful hits by 2.3--3.4\%, and increase L2 misses by 4,225--6,335. This is a controlled negative result: adding a larger learned gate to the already-strong streamer's spatial candidate policy is not automatically beneficial.
\item \textbf{Relation to the completed stride experiment:} h8's absolute IPC (0.43134) is higher than stride h16's 0.37090, but those are different normal candidate mechanisms and traffic levels. It is not evidence that the streamer LSTM is a better neural model. The correct conclusions are: the stride-matched h16 beat stride; the streamer-matched LSTM did not beat streamer.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Interpretation and next decision}
Streamer itself is the source of the large performance gain: offline streamer improves IPC by 17.26\% over no prefetch and reduces L2 demand misses by 92.37\%. The LSTM was restricted to selecting from the same streamer candidates, so a gate can only win by removing harmful candidates without deleting useful ones. It did not do that on this window. Do not add capacity or tune solely to overcome the 0.00016 gap: h8 already behaves almost identically to streamer, while larger widths move in the wrong direction.

These counters are sufficient to diagnose the proximate failure: h8 loses 239 useful lines for only six fewer late misses, while larger models trade coverage for higher request-level yield. They are not sufficient to reconstruct exact hardware prefetch accuracy or lead time. That would require accepted/merged/rejected request counts, evicted-before-use lines, duplicate suppression, and a per-request issue-to-demand distance distribution. The negative matched-input conclusion itself is nevertheless supported by the current data.

This is a useful negative control, not a failed provenance result. The next independent matched-input study should use AMPM's different page-access bitmap input contract. Its result must be interpreted separately; no streamer model, list, threshold, or capacity may be selected for AMPM.
