\section*{Metrics: formula first}
Let $D=404{,}219$ be post-warmup demand L2 loads, $M$ the method's demand L2 misses, $R$ replayed prefetch-request events, $U$ useful-prefetch demand hits, $L$ demand misses merged into an in-flight prefetch, and $M_0=203{,}131$ no-prefetch L2 demand misses.
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{MR}_{L2}=100\frac{M}{D}}
\qquad
\boxed{A_{\mathrm{proxy}}=100\frac{U}{R}}
\qquad
\boxed{C=100\frac{U}{M_0}}
\qquad
\boxed{T_{\mathrm{late}}=10^5\frac{L}{R}}
\]
Because $D$ is identical across these matched rows, miss rate and miss reduction contain the same ordering information:
\[
\mathrm{MissReduction}=100\frac{M_0-M}{M_0},
\qquad \mathrm{MR}_{L2,0}=100\frac{M_0}{D}=50.2527\%.
\]
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
Metric & What it measures in this experiment & Direction \\
\midrule
$\mathrm{MR}_{L2}$ & Final demand-miss fraction after all prefetch effects. This is the most direct cache-outcome metric shared by live and replayed methods. & Lower \\
$A_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ & Observed useful-hit yield per request event. It is \emph{not} strict hardware prefetch accuracy because accepted fills, duplicates, unused lines, and evictions are not separated. & Higher \\
$C$ & Useful demand hits as a fraction of the no-prefetch miss opportunity. & Higher \\
$T_{\mathrm{late}}$ & Late merges per 100k request events. It is a late-density proxy, \emph{not} a lead-time distribution; suppressing requests can lower it. & Lower \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section*{Data $\rightarrow$ insight map}
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.23\textwidth}p{0.34\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Question & Direct evidence & Supported insight \\
\midrule
Why does h8 miss AMPM? & L2 misses rise by 1,734; $\mathrm{MR}_{L2}$ rises from 4.218\% to 4.647\%; $U$ and coverage fall by the same 1,734 opportunities while IPC falls 0.00138. Requests fall only 0.492\%. & The closest gate still removes useful service. The direct miss-rate increase confirms that coverage loss, not request-volume reduction, tracks the IPC loss.\\
Is timeliness the limiting factor? & h8 late density improves from 46.699 to 31.603; wider models reduce it as low as 1.141 while IPC gets worse. & \textbf{No.} Lower late density does not compensate for lost useful coverage and can partly reflect request suppression.\\
Does more LSTM capacity help? & h8 is best; h16--h128 use more parameters but lose 1.62--15.18 coverage points relative to AMPM. & No monotonic capacity saturation is observed. The bottleneck is policy/label/threshold behavior, not insufficient hidden width.\\
Did stateful TBPTT fix the gap? & Recurrent-state contract is now matched and the run passes, yet no width crosses AMPM. & The previous state mismatch was a real methodology bug, but it was not the cause of the AMPM performance gap.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section*{What these counters can and cannot prove}
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
Claim type & Supported now & Still required for a causal claim \\
\midrule
Performance & IPC, cycles, L2 demand hits/misses, and matched replay establish the measured ordering. & Multiple traces, seeds, and a fresh held-out window for generalization.\\
Accuracy & $U/R$ compares request-event yield. & Accepted/filled unique prefetches, duplicate rejection, unused eviction, and pollution counters for strict accuracy.\\
Timeliness & Late merges and late density compare one failure mode. & Prefetch issue/fill/demand cycles and lead-time histograms to distinguish too-early, on-time, and too-late requests.\\
Root cause & h8 loses useful hits and coverage even while late density improves. & Bandwidth/MSHR pressure, cache pollution, critical-miss stall cycles, and per-target classification errors.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
