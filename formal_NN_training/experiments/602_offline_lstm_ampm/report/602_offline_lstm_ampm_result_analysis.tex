\section*{Status and experiment contract}
\textbf{Status: PASS.} This is an isolated, single-trace, matched-input offline experiment. Colab trains a one-layer LSTM on the 0--20M no-prefetch address stream, uses a chronological fit/calibration split, and performs causal offline inference after a disjoint 20M--25M guard stream initializes state. The 25M--50M stream is used only for evaluation-list generation. ChampSim replays both the deterministic offline-AMPM list and each offline-LSTM list with the same keyed PC--line--occurrence \texttt{ListReplayer}, followed by the same 25M warmup and 25M measured window.

The pinned AMPM configuration has 64 LRU page trackers, one 64-bit accessed-offset bitmap per page, maximum delta 16, prediction/prefetch degree 4, and its prefetch buffer disabled. Both policies receive the current cache-line address and causal prior address history. The LSTM derives the equivalent page bitmap/LRU state, current offset, tracker-hit state, and AMPM candidate delta/offset/rank from that same stream. PC is replay identity only; hit/miss, cycle, queue state, metadata, and future evaluation rows are forbidden.

The primary comparison is \texttt{offline\_ampm} versus \texttt{offline\_lstm\_h<width>}. Live AMPM is a validated general reference, not the primary offline comparator. All model tags passed canonical decompressed-content SHA256 provenance checks.

\section*{What ``accuracy'' and ``timeliness'' mean here}
The event logger does not expose strict hardware prefetch accuracy. We therefore report \emph{observed useful-hit yield}, defined as useful-prefetch demand hits divided by post-warmup prefetch-request events. This is only a diagnostic proxy: a request may be rejected, merged, duplicated, or evicted before use, and those outcomes are not individually counted. Coverage is useful hits divided by the 203,131 no-prefetch L2 demand misses. Late/request is a timeliness proxy; a complete timeliness study would need the issue-to-demand lead-time distribution.

These counters are sufficient to determine whether a gate preserved useful coverage while reducing late requests. They are not sufficient to claim exact physical prefetch accuracy, cache pollution, queue pressure, or inference-cost effects.

\section*{Why the closest model still loses}
The closest point is h8, but it is not a crossing. Relative to offline AMPM, h8:
\begin{itemize}
\item removes 6,219 requests (0.489\%);
\item loses 1,718 useful-prefetch demand hits (0.923\%);
\item lowers observed useful-hit yield from 14.629\% to 14.565\%;
\item lowers coverage by 0.846 percentage points;
\item reduces late misses from 594 to 399, a reduction of 195 (32.8\%);
\item adds exactly 1,718 L2 demand misses and loses 0.00137 IPC (0.318\%).
\end{itemize}
The exact useful-hit loss/L2-miss increase correspondence gives a clear proximate explanation: the gate discards useful AMPM candidates. Its 195 fewer late misses are too small to compensate for 1,718 lost useful lines. Thus h8 is neither more accurate by observed useful-hit yield nor better balanced in coverage/timeliness than offline AMPM.

\section*{Capacity behavior}
Increasing capacity does not approach a saturation crossing. h16 is lower than h8. h32 and h64 are non-monotonic, but both are far below AMPM. h128 removes 9.87\% of requests, loses 16.95\% of useful hits, lowers yield to 13.481\%, lowers coverage by 15.52 percentage points, and adds 31,533 L2 misses. Its late count falls to 12, but IPC falls by 3.37\% relative to offline AMPM. Reducing late events by suppressing useful candidates is not a performance win.

The data therefore reject the hypothesis that widening this candidate gate is enough. A same-input redesign should keep the AMPM candidate set and address-derived state fixed, but change the learning objective toward coverage-preserving utility/ranking and calibrate request budgets without selecting on the evaluation IPC.

\section*{What is established and what remains missing}
The present data are sufficient to establish a fair negative result on this seed/window: no tested LSTM capacity beats offline AMPM, and the measured deficit is associated with lost useful coverage that dominates improved late counts. They are also sufficient to show that model size alone is not the solution.

They are not sufficient for a general hardware claim or a complete causal decomposition. A later confirmation should add accepted/merged/rejected requests, duplicate suppression, filled-but-unused and evicted-before-use lines, PQ/MSHR occupancy, DRAM traffic, and issue-to-demand lead-time distributions. Any redesigned winner must be fixed before evaluation and then confirmed on a fresh held-out 602 window and additional seeds.
