\section*{Status and experiment contract}
This is a single-trace, offline experiment. Colab trains a one-layer LSTM on an earlier no-prefetch training prefix and performs causal sequential inference on a disjoint no-prefetch evaluation stream. It exports keyed PC--line--occurrence replay lists. ChampSim replays both the fixed offline-stride list and each offline-LSTM list using the same \texttt{ListReplayer} transport.

The shared evaluation information is current PC, current cache-line address, and causal prior PC/address history. The model does not receive hit/miss, cycle, queue state, metadata, or future evaluation rows. The primary comparison is \texttt{offline\_stride} versus \texttt{offline\_lstm\_h<width>}. Live stride is a validated general reference only.

All eight simulations completed with the same 25M warmup and 25M measured window (25,000,003 reported instructions). The final analyzer reports \texttt{PASS}, an empty failure list, and \texttt{fair\_comparison\_claim\_allowed=true}. For every model tag, it verified the original Colab-input gzip hash and then proved that the original and current decompressed CSV contents are identical. No Colab or ChampSim rerun is required.

\section*{Accuracy, coverage, and timeliness diagnosis}
The available counters do not contain a direct hardware prefetch-accuracy field. We therefore report \emph{observed useful-hit yield}, defined as useful-prefetch demand hits divided by post-warmup prefetch-request events. This is a diagnostic proxy, not strict accuracy: a request can be rejected, merged, duplicated, or evicted before use, and those outcomes are not separately counted here. Coverage is useful hits divided by the 203,131 no-prefetch L2 demand misses. The late/request ratio is also only a timeliness proxy; suppressing requests can reduce the absolute late count without improving the timing of retained requests.

Offline stride has 34,850 useful hits from 60,255 requests, an observed useful-hit yield of 57.84\%, coverage of 17.16\%, and 165 late misses (0.274\% of requests). The measured h16 crossing has 13,700 useful hits from 33,891 requests: its yield is only 40.42\%, coverage is 6.74\%, and its 144 late misses are 0.425\% of requests. It also has 21,151 more L2 demand misses than offline stride (189,432 versus 168,281). Thus the measured accuracy, coverage, and timeliness proxies do \emph{not} explain h16's small IPC advantage.

The correct conclusion is limited: h16 measured 0.00074 higher IPC on this one seed/window, but the current counters cannot attribute that difference to a better prefetch decision. Possible unmeasured mechanisms include cache pollution, duplicate or rejected requests, bandwidth/queue pressure, and memory-level parallelism; ordinary run-to-run or window variation is also not ruled out.

\section*{What the data supports}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Best and first measured crossing:} h16 has 1,729 trainable parameters and IPC 0.37090. It exceeds offline stride by 0.00074 IPC, or 0.200\% relative to offline stride, and exceeds no prefetch by 0.788\%.
\item \textbf{Not a monotonic saturation curve:} h32 is only 0.00017 above stride; h8, h64, and h128 are below stride. Therefore h16 is the current \emph{best measured capacity point}, not proof that more parameters monotonically improve performance or that a final saturation point is known.
\item \textbf{Counter interpretation:} h16 replayed 33,891 requests, 43.75\% fewer than offline stride's 60,255, but its useful-hit yield (40.42\% versus 57.84\%), coverage (6.74\% versus 17.16\%), late/request ratio (0.425\% versus 0.274\%), and L2 demand misses are all worse. Do not describe h16 as more accurate or more timely from these counters. Its IPC crossing is measured but mechanistically unresolved.
\item \textbf{Transport validation:} offline stride (0.37016) closely matches active live stride (0.37006). The old inactive live-stride row is not used.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How to interpret and confirm h16}
The capacity sweep reused one evaluation stream to compare widths. Treat h16 as an \emph{exploratory selection}, not a final general claim. Before reporting LSTM beats stride beyond this sweep, keep h16 fixed and confirm it on a fresh held-out 602 window and at least one additional random seed. The next analysis question is not add more parameters, but why h16's more selective list produces a small IPC gain despite lower useful-hit count.

To explain that mechanism, a confirmation run should add accepted/merged/rejected prefetch counts, filled-but-unused and evicted-before-use lines, duplicate suppression, PQ/MSHR occupancy, DRAM traffic, and a lead-time distribution. The present data are sufficient to report the matched-input IPC crossing and to reject an accuracy/timeliness explanation; they are not sufficient to claim why the crossing occurred or that it generalizes.
